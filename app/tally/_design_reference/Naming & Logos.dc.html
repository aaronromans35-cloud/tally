Marks

Shown at app-icon size and in a header, using “Tally” as placeholder text.
A · Tally marks
Four strokes and a slash — the oldest streak counter there is. Reads as five, which is the weekly habit goal.
B · Steps
Three rungs climbing. Works for Rung, Stack, or Tally. Scales down to a favicon without losing the idea.
C · Squircle (current)
The safe one. Neutral, obviously an app icon, and the inner square can animate into a checkmark on log-in.
D · The notch
A block with a bite taken out — one notch cut for every day logged. Made for the name Notch.
E · Progress ring
The mark IS the streak — it fills as you log days. Downside: rings are everywhere in fitness apps.
F · Week grid
Lifted straight from the streak screen, so the icon and the product are the same object. Feels most like software.
T
G · Monogram
Cheapest to execute and hardest to get wrong. Only works if the name survives — swap the letter, keep the system.
H · Coin stack
Literal and warm. Reads instantly as money to a 15-year-old, which the abstract marks don't.
I · Check
The single gesture the whole product asks for. Reads as “done” in every language and needs no explanation.
J · Fill level
A vessel filling up. The icon can literally rise with your savings rate — the only mark here that is also a live gauge.
K · The sill
A ledge with something resting on it. Built for the name Sill, and the warmest shape in the set.
L · Growth bars
The most literally financial mark here. Credible to a bank partner, least distinctive to a 16-year-old.
M · Coin outline
A dashed coin — money that isn't fixed yet. The dashes double as days, closing the loop as the week completes.
N · Pyramid
Foundation first, then the small stuff on top — the order the product actually teaches. Works for Stack or Grain.
